# Your seat is which room you are in

The **Section network** is a directory for holders: each can publish a card, introduce themselves, and talk in their cabin's room. What your seat decides is **which cabin you belong to** — and the rule is one line, applied everywhere:

{% hint style="success" %}
**Each cabin sees only itself.** Your seat opens your own section: its cards, its people's inboxes and its room. Every other section — in front of you or behind you — keeps its contact details and its conversations to itself.
{% endhint %}

## Who can read what

| | Who can read it |
| --- | --- |
| **A holder's name and role** | Every holder signed in to the directory. The roster belongs to the whole aircraft. |
| **A holder's contact details** — email, website, LinkedIn | Their own section, and nobody else. |
| **A conversation between two holders** | The two wallets on it, and nobody else. |
| **A cabin's room** | That cabin, and nobody else. |
| **The PA** | Everyone aboard, the cargo hold included. |

## Writing goes exactly as far as reading

If you can read somebody's card, you can introduce yourself to them — so you can write to anybody in your own section, and to nobody outside it. The **cargo hold** is not a cabin: a wallet without a seat is on no roster, its card is not shown, and it has no room to talk in.

{% hint style="info" %}
These rules are enforced by the server, not just by the page. A card from another cabin arrives without its contact details, and a message to another cabin is refused: _"That holder is in another cabin. Introductions stay within your own."_
{% endhint %}
